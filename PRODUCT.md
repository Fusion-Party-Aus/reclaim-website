# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Three overlapping audiences, all entering from different vectors:

- **Disillusioned progressives**: ex-Labor or ex-Greens voters who sense those parties have calcified. They're skeptical of new parties by default and need to see that Fusion is real, organised, and serious before they give it their vote.
- **Curious first-timers**: younger or first-time Victorian voters who've heard about Fusion through a friend or social post. They're open but need a fast, clear answer to "what is this and why should I care?"
- **Tech-adjacent activists**: people who care about evidence-based policy, digital rights, rationalism, and effective governance — Fusion's natural ideological base. They'll read the policies closely and notice if the details are thin.

All three land on different pages. All three need to leave with a clear next action.

## Product Purpose

The Fusion Party Victoria website is the party's primary conversion engine ahead of the 2026 Victorian state elections. Three interconnected success milestones:

1. **Candidate recruitment**: 88 electorates need candidates. The site must make running feel achievable, not intimidating.
2. **Member threshold**: Hit the formal registration number required to stand as a party. Every visit is a potential signup.
3. **Policy legitimacy**: Be taken seriously as an evidence-based party — not a protest vote, not a single-issue fringe group. Specific, costed, credible policy.

Success is all three. They are not in competition: credible policy attracts members, members produce candidates, candidates enable elections.

## Brand Personality

Bold, honest, urgent.

- **Bold**: The aesthetic is confrontational by design. Neo-brutalism is not decorative — it is the political position made visual. No softening.
- **Honest**: Real numbers, real gaps, real costs. The movement metrics show 12/88 electorates filled. That is not hidden; it is the rallying point.
- **Urgent**: 2026 is not far. The site should communicate that this window is open now and won't stay open forever.

Tone of voice: direct, specific, without condescension. The copy talks to adults who can handle complexity. No filler words. No corporate hedging.

## Anti-references

- **Labor/Liberal establishment polish**: corporate-smooth, navy-and-red websites designed to reassure, not inspire. Any design that looks like it was made by a comms department is wrong.
- **Greens warmth**: earnest-activist, warm-toned, slightly-amateurish grassroots aesthetic. The sincerity is right; the visual timidity is not.
- **Startup SaaS landing page**: hero-with-metrics, gradient-accent, glassmorphism product marketing. Fusion is not selling software. The design must not look like a growth-hacking playbook.
- **Protest movement ephemeral**: handwritten fonts, scanned textures, zine aesthetics. Looks like it won't survive the next news cycle. Fusion needs to signal permanence and institutional seriousness alongside its anti-establishment energy.

## Design Principles

1. **The aesthetic is the argument.** Neo-brutalist design is not a style choice — it is a statement that Fusion rejects the smooth, managed look of establishment politics. Every visual decision should reinforce that rejection. If something looks like it belongs on a major party site, it is wrong.

2. **Show the honest state.** Radical transparency over reassurance. Display the real numbers — how many electorates are filled, how many members exist, how much has been raised. Incompleteness is not a weakness to hide; it is the open gap that invites people to act.

3. **Every screen ends in a clear action.** The site exists to convert: to a member, a volunteer, a candidate. The information hierarchy on any page should always lead to something the reader can do right now. Dead ends are wasted conversions.

4. **Evidence over adjective.** Specific beats general. A costed policy beats a vague commitment. A filled electorate map beats a slogan about representation. Credibility is in the specifics — design should foreground data, not decorate it.

5. **Practice what you preach.** A progressive evidence-based party should run an exemplary product. WCAG AAA accessibility, reduced motion respect, real performance — not as compliance boxes, but as proof that Fusion holds itself to the standards it sets for others.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target: WCAG AAA where feasible.
- Reduced motion: all animated elements (scroll progress, movement metrics, viral share interactions) must respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Given the extent of animation features, this is non-negotiable.
- Color contrast: all text must meet AAA contrast ratios against their backgrounds. The existing palette (Magenta, Mint, Yellow on white/black) can achieve this but requires careful implementation.
- Screen reader support: semantic HTML first. Interactive components (movement metrics, share buttons) require full keyboard navigability and appropriate ARIA labeling.
- No dark mode currently — the light palette is contrast-dependent and must not be inverted without a full dark-mode specification.
