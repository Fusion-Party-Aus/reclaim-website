THE PROBLEM — WHY NOW

FUSION HAS ALWAYS KNOWN WHAT IT IS.
THE PROBLEM IS SAYING IT OUT LOUD.

Fusion has had clear, costed, rigorous policy since its inception. People inside the party understand the mechanisms — the PPP contracts, the tax structures, the infrastructure ownership arrangements — better than most of the major parties. The analysis has never been the problem.

The problem is the gap between what is clear internally and what is legible externally. Ask ten Fusion members to describe the party to a stranger and you get ten different answers — all of them accurate, none of them the same. That is not a policy problem. That is a brand problem.

Reclaim's biggest opportunity is to close that gap permanently.

When the internal team is aligned — when every member, candidate, and volunteer uses the same frame — the external message becomes coherent automatically. You don't need to train everyone on the same answer. You give everyone the same question: Victoria owes $199 billion. Who's collecting the rent?

Reclaim is not just a campaign identity. It is an internal alignment tool. A shared language that makes every conversation — at a doorstep, in a media interview, on social media — start from the same place and land in the same direction.

THE EXTERNAL PROBLEM
"SO WHAT DOES FUSION ACTUALLY STAND FOR?"

The question every Fusion member has been asked and struggled to answer quickly. Not because the answer doesn't exist — but because it was too long, too technical, or too different depending on who you asked.

THE INTERNAL PROBLEM
IDENTITY FRAGMENTATION

The most futurist party. The techno-Greens. The evidence-based party. The infrastructure party. All true. None of them the same sentence. Without a unifying frame, every piece of content risks pointing in a slightly different direction.

WHAT RECLAIM DOES
ONE FRAME. EVERYTHING FITS.

The futurism fits — reclaiming the capacity to build the future. The evidence-base fits — the receipts are the point. The infrastructure focus fits — that's what we're reclaiming. Reclaim doesn't replace any of it. It names the thread that connects all of it.

00 — THE STRATEGIC CHOICE

STOP GIVING THE RIGHT
THE LANGUAGE OF STRENGTH.

Progressive movements attract people who care about harm, who notice unfairness, who do not want politics to become cruel. That instinct is one of the best things about progressive politics.

But that same instinct becomes a weakness when it turns into fear of touching anything the Right has ever used. Words like strength. Security. Ambition. Freedom. Reclaim. Too often, progressives hear those words and flinch — treating them as contaminated because conservatives used them badly. So we retreat into softer, procedural language.

This is how we surrender political territory. This is not principled. This is strategic self-harm.

A word is not right-wing because the Right uses it. A message is not dangerous because it is simple, forceful, and emotionally legible. The question is not whether a phrase has been used by the Right. The question is what we use it to do.

If anti-democratic actors wrap themselves in democratic language, the answer is not to retreat. The answer is to expose the fraud by using those words honestly, with policies that actually mean them.

Purity politics that avoids every tainted word is not a path to moral victory. It is a path to electoral failure.

WHAT THE RIGHT MEANS BY "RECLAIM"

A vague cultural grievance. Reclaim from immigrants, from modernity, from an imagined outgroup. An emotion with no mechanism. A return to a mythologised past that never existed.

Fear dressed up as toughness.

WHAT WE MEAN BY "RECLAIM"

A specific, costed, forward-looking programme. Reclaim the public infrastructure that was privatised under terms that demonstrably failed. Not from a group of people — from a contract clause. One we have read. All 847 pages of it.

Ambition with receipts.

WHY THIS WORD SPECIFICALLY

Reclaim was used by a serious progressive left-wing group in Australia well before the fringe right picked it up. The case for reclaiming "Reclaim" is stronger than the case for ceding it. We should take it back before fringe forces have time to cement their claim. And tactically: using this language will capture a portion of disaffected voters who would otherwise, with nothing but a ballot paper, pick something like One Nation.

The only thing better than winning once is winning twice — by winning a vote for Reclaim and taking that vote away from One Nation.

01 — WHAT WE ARE

FIVE THINGS AT ONCE.
NO CONTRADICTION.

01
MATERIALIST

Reclaim starts with the material world — the trains that are late, the rent that is too high, the contract that no one read. Not feelings about these things. The things themselves. Who owns them. What they cost. Who benefits.

The analysis starts from material conditions and works backwards to cause. Not from ideology forward to evidence. The evidence comes first — every time.

02
EVIDENCE-
BASED

Every claim has a source. Every number has a citation. Every policy has a cost. This is not a performance of rigour — it is the actual standard. Unlike other populist movements, we have policy published on the website. If you cannot cite it, you do not publish it.

"We costed it. Here's what you'd save." — the sentence that ends every policy argument
03
POPULIST

People are not living in a calm political environment. Living standards are under pressure. Housing feels impossible. Work feels less secure. Institutions feel remote. People are right to be angry — the question is where that anger goes.

Our job is not to drain the anger out of politics. Our job is to direct it toward the systems that are actually failing people. The populists' offer is simple: your life is harder because someone beneath you or beside you has taken something that should be yours. That is a lie. But it is a lie with emotional force.

THE CRITICAL DISTINCTION

Populism that generates hatred toward outgroups is not our model. We do not need a scapegoat because we have a contract clause. You may blame "the government" or "successive governments" — but people shall never be scapegoated. This is both against our values and unnecessary.

04
PROGRESSIVE
+
FUTURIST

Fusion is the most futurist political party in Australia. Reclaim does not change that — it extends it. We are not reclaiming the past. We are reclaiming the capacity to build the future. You cannot fund world-class public transport, clean energy grids, and universal housing on the current PPP model. That is a statement of fiscal arithmetic, not nostalgia.

Australia should be ambitious enough to build cheap clean energy, world-class public schools, decent homes, strong industries, and communities that are not left to rot. That is not the past. It is the future we should have had already.

PUBLIC WHERE IT WORKS

Natural monopoly infrastructure. Trains, hospitals, water, energy grids. Where the market has demonstrably failed and public provision demonstrably works better.

COMPETITIVE WHERE IT DOESN'T

Not ideological. Empirical. The question is always: what does the evidence say produces better outcomes for the person paying for it?

05
OPINIONATED

Reclaim has opinions. Clear, direct, costed opinions. We do not triangulate for the sake of appearing reasonable. We do not mistake softness for virtue. We do not assume that the safest language is the most ethical language. Sometimes the safest language is just the language that changes nothing.

Not centrist. Not "both sides." Firmly positioned. Willing to say the quiet part clearly — and then show you the document it came from.

02 — WHAT WE ARE NOT

THE LINES WE
DON'T CROSS.

NOT CENTRIST

Centrism is a position, not an absence of one. Reclaim doesn't seek the middle of a broken argument. When privatisation demonstrably fails, we don't say "there are merits on both sides." We say it demonstrably failed, and here are the auditor-general's numbers.

NOT ANTI-IMMIGRANT

When we say "reclaim our economy" we mean from a model of privatised infrastructure without accountability — not from people. The housing crisis is a planning and supply failure. The transport crisis is a PPP pricing failure. Immigration is not the mechanism. The contract is. We will be specific about this before we launch.

NOT NOSTALGIC

This is not a campaign about returning to something that was better. It is about building something that has not existed yet — a fully funded, publicly accountable, technically excellent set of public institutions. If your politics is to drag the country backwards, you are not strong. You are afraid. The future is the point.

NOT OUTGROUP POPULISM

We do not achieve reach by generating hatred toward a group of people. No class enemy, no ethnic scapegoat, no coded "elites" when what we mean is a specific contractual arrangement. The mechanism is always the villain. Mechanisms can be changed. That is the difference between politics and theatre.

NOT SENTIMENT

"Too many Victorians are left behind" is not a Reclaim sentence. "34% of renters spend more than 40% of income on housing, and the Cross Yarra Partnership contract allows fare increases indexed to operator margin" is. The difference between political theatre and political argument — and we intend to be the latter.

THE POLITICAL MOMENT

GOOD COMPANY ON THE LEFT.
DIRECT OPPOSITION ON THE RIGHT.

Australia's political climate is not calm. The right is energised, organised, and increasingly willing to borrow from the worst of what is happening internationally — the scapegoating, the institutional erosion, the culture-war distraction from the policy failures that are actually making people's lives harder.

In this climate, Reclaim is not a neutral brand. It takes a side. It is in good company on the left — aligned with the progressive values, the democratic institutions, and the public-interest economics that the Australian left has historically fought for.

And it is in almost complete opposition to the right. That is deliberate. That is the point.

The right's offer right now is: your life is harder because of immigrants, minorities, "elites," or the ABC. It is a lie packaged as courage. It is fear dressed up as toughness. It is nostalgia for a past that never existed, sold as a plan for the future.

Reclaim's offer is: your life is harder because of a specific contract clause, a specific privatisation decision, a specific failure of public accountability. Here is the document. Here is the mechanism. Here is what we would change and what it would cost.

Same emotional terrain. Completely different destination.

03B — THE COLOUR

WHY PURPLE?
BECAUSE NO ONE ELSE OWNS IT.

Purple was already Fusion's colour — it's in the rings mark, in every piece of material we've ever made. We're not inventing a justification; we're documenting the one that was always there.

In Australian politics, every major party has a colour. Labor has red. Liberal has blue. The Greens have green. Every one carries decades of association — with a class, a region, a tribe. Purple is uncontested territory. No one else has claimed it, no one has loaded it with broken promises. It was ours before we had a reason. Now we have one.

And the reason turns out to be exactly right.

Red plus blue makes purple. You could read this as centrism — taking a bit from each side, splitting the difference. That would be the wrong reading entirely.

We are not finding the centre of the left-right axis. We are refusing to accept that the axis is the right frame. We take what works from wherever it works. The result is not a compromise. It is a synthesis — and syntheses are more than the sum of their inputs.

Purple is what you get when you stop choosing a side and start asking what actually works.
